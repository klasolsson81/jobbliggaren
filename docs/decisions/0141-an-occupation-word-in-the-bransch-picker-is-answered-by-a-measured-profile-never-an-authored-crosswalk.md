## Context

In "Ny smart bevakning" the bransch picker was a dead end for occupation words: `systemutvecklare`
ended in *"Inga träffar"* and `sjuksköterska` in ten code rows. Users think in occupations; the
watch is built on SNI because the company register carries nothing else — a company is linked to an
occupation only through its ads.

The obvious remedy, an authored occupation → SNI alias list, is closed. `tools/sni-aliases`
condition 5 refuses cross-industry occupations with `systemutvecklare` as the worked example, and
#560 bind 4 states the ground: *"company branches (SNI) and job-ad occupation groups (SSYK/JobTech)
are different taxonomies and mapping them is a claim the data does not support."* The measurement
shows why an authored list would also have been wrong: `systemutvecklare`'s largest employer group is
**78 Bemanning** (27 %), not 62 Dataprogrammering (17 %).

So the answer is counted, never authored: for every occupation group, where do the employers who
actually advertised for it sit, by SNI huvudgrupp, in our own ads.

## Decision

**1. A derived profile, occupation group × SNI huvudgrupp × ad count, is materialised by a Worker
job out of `job_ads ⋈ company_register`.** The tables (`occupation_division_profiles`,
`occupation_division_profile_runs`) are Infrastructure-internal and never a `DbSet` on
`IAppDbContext`, exactly as ADR 0139's tables are and for the same reason: the join against the
register is the one thing that cannot be done anywhere else, and the register stays off the port
(DPIA C-D4 / M-C5). The whole aggregate runs server-side in one statement, so no org.nr ever reaches
application code — which is why `OrganizationNumberSurfacingGuardTests.RawOrgNrReadingSourcePaths`
is deliberately not extended, and the day an intermediate step reads one, it is.

**2. ADR 0139's GDPR half does not transfer, and that is measured, not assumed.** The profile
carries no `user_id`, no `criterion_id` and no `organization_number`; nothing in it is about a person.
It therefore joins neither `MappedPlaintextExposureRegistry`'s person-grained tables nor the accepted
restore-exposure list, needs no FK cascade and no erasure oracle. Its three text columns are classified
`NoPersonalData` / `NotRecruiterData` beside the taxonomy tables, and the Art. 30 entry for smart
bevakning records the contrast. `security-auditor` confirmed the non-attributability on the PR.

**3. The window is every ad we have seen — `Active` and `Archived` — as a positive allow-list, never
a `<>`.** Klas's decision (2026-09-14): where an occupation's employers are is a structural fact and
should rest on the widest honest base, not swing with the week's active ads. `Erased` is the Art. 17
tombstone (#842) and is excluded by construction; a future status is excluded until someone adds it
to the list (JobAdSearchComposition's #864 D4 rule, applied to a raw read). The register side takes
the same shape: `Active` and `Deregistered` both count — a company since deregistered *was* the
employer on those ads, and the profile is backward-looking, unlike a watch (CTO D6) — and a third
register status lands in the not-in-register bucket rather than in a published number. The read is
declared as a `RawSqlNonReach` in `JobAdLifecycleReadRegistry` (ADR 0113) with that decision in words.

**4. Employers outside the register are their own bucket, never dropped.** `LEFT JOIN`, with two
non-digit sentinels in storage (`--` not in register, `-?` in the register without an SNI code) that
never cross the Application boundary: the port maps them to a typed `NotInRegisterAdCount` scalar
beside — never inside — the division list, so the bucket is structurally unpickable. Measured: 3 037
of 83 280 ads (3.6 %) sit in it; the second sentinel measured zero and is counted on the run row so
that a bucket that starts filling is visible rather than silent.

**5. The job is its own recurring job on its own cron, clock-padded after the daily snapshot —
not a watermark, not a chain.** `build-occupation-division-profile`, `OccupationDivisionProfile:CadenceCron`
default `35 3 * * *` UTC, `Enabled` default true, own section (a cadence tied to another feature's
flag runs as often as that flag is true — security-auditor Major 3 on ADR 0139). The issue's *"once
per completed ingest run"* is unsatisfiable as written: `job_ads` has **two** writers (the snapshot
daily, the stream every ten minutes), so a watermark on the snapshot's audit row fires exactly as
often as a daily cron while claiming to track ingest, and one on every `JobAdsSynced` row rebuilds
144 times a day for a distribution that moves by fractions of a percent. A rebuild is 163 ms, so
over-running is cheap; the claim, not the cost, decided it (CTO D1). The tables are `ANALYZE`d once
per completed run that wrote rows (AGENTS.md §3.6, all three conditions evaluated). Hangfire's
default retry is kept: the next scheduled attempt is 24 h away.

**6. Thresholds are read-time policy over a full measurement, and the floor is derived from the
threshold, not chosen.** The store holds all ~5 100 rows; the read applies the issue's ≥ 5 % share
cut and refuses a group under **21 ads** — the smallest N at which a single ad cannot clear 5 % alone
(1/20 = 5.0 % does, 1/21 = 4.76 % does not). Below it a "≥ 5 % list" is an enumeration of individual
employers wearing the grammar of a distribution. `MinimumAdsPerGroup` is a derived property of
`MinimumSharePercent`, never a second setting. Measured effect: 310 of 395 groups render, 85 are
refused honestly — Klas chose the floor with a visible count over the issue's bare shares. The read
also refuses a profile older than two cadence intervals (48 h): one missed run is transient, two is a
broken job. Three states, never a zero for "unknown" (`Profiled` / `TooFewAds` / `NotProfiled`).

**7. The typed word is resolved by the delivered `IOccupationCodeDeriver` — reuse, not a second
matcher.** The brief priced this as a new consumer of the frozen occupation-name → group crosswalk;
it is not: the deriver is already a ratified live-read consumer (senior-cto-advisor Decision 2,
2026-06-14). Its stemmed, spread-gated matching cannot be reimplemented faithfully in TypeScript,
which is also why the read path is a `q`-shaped, client-debounced endpoint on the picker's own
family (`GET /me/company-watch-criteria/occupation-divisions?q=`, own rate-limit bucket beside
`preview-count`, `private, no-store`) rather than a bulk payload — and why `/reference`, already
22.5 kB gzip inlined into five documents against ADR 0045's 30 kB budget, was never a carrier.
`SearchSynonyms:Occupations` is not consulted: it is another bounded context's map, bound in the Api's
configuration only, and it adds nothing the deriver does not find. Measured on the live snapshot:
`systemutvecklare` → 1 group, `sjuksköterska` → 2.

**8. Several occupation groups for one word are a choice the user makes, never a pick made for her.**
AGENTS.md §5 lists SSYK derivation without confirmation as an anti-pattern (ADR 0040 Beslut 4), so
the block lists the group names, the user picks one, and that group's map renders; one group skips
the step. Klas saw the extra step and accepted it (2026-09-14). Ticking a huvudgrupp calls the
picker's existing `onToggle(leafCodes)` — exactly what picking it from the tree does — so no new
criterion axis exists and `CompanyWatchCriterion` is untouched.

**9. Four properties keep this a measurement and not the crosswalk bind 4 forbids, and they are
structural.** It never preselects; the count always travels beside the share (`48 % (412 annonser)`
— the middle dot is bound as the axis separator, CTO 2026-09-14); it never collapses to a single
division; the not-in-register bucket stays visible. The window is disclosed once, in the block's
ingress (Klas's wording: *"X är ett yrke, inte en bransch. I de N annonser vi sett för yrket finns
arbetsgivarna i:"*), never per row, because `annonser` two clicks away means live ads. Bind 4 stands
and is not superseded.

**10. Two PRs.** Part 1 (the SNI code stops leading a filtered row, no schema — #1726, merged
`fe95e886`) first, so the row form was settled before new rows were built against it; part 2 (this
ADR's substance) carries the migration hotspot.

## Consequences

- A user typing an occupation word gets a measured map of where its employers are and can tick a
  huvudgrupp into the watch; the watch stays SNI-based and the register stays the source that also
  finds companies not advertising right now.
- One more recurring job, two more tables, one migration (single-owner hotspot, §6.5).
- Staleness is at most one cadence, and past two the surface says it does not know.
- The deriver's whole-word, spread-gated matching means a generic word (`chef`) yields no block, and
  a word yields nothing until it is complete — accepted as the honest behaviour, not engineered around.

## Alternatives considered

- **An authored occupation → SNI alias list** — refused before this ADR by condition 5 and bind 4.
- **A level-triggered watermark on the snapshot's audit row (D1 b)** — false fidelity, see Decision 5.
- **Chaining from the ingest job (D1 c)** — the edge-triggered Application → Worker handoff ADR 0139
  clause (ii) already rejected.
- **A new token-aware matcher over group labels (D2 M1)** — a third matcher beside a delivered one,
  and it fails the issue's own acceptance (the block appears mid-word and vanishes on the last letter).
- **A bulk endpoint with matching in the client (D3 B)** — reimplements Snowball stemming in TS;
  two authorities for one question, diverging by construction.
- **Folding into `/reference` (D3 C)** — the document-size budget, five consumer documents, and
  `CriterionReferenceAliasTests.RealAsset_CarriesNoOccupationIdentifier_SoItCannotBeACrosswalk`.
- **Largest group / top-K / a union distribution when a word resolves to several groups (D2b)** —
  each is an auto-selection or an opaque aggregate over an unconfirmed derivation; §5 rules them out.
- **A statistically derived floor** — every number available today would be chosen; 21 is the only
  one the threshold itself produces.
