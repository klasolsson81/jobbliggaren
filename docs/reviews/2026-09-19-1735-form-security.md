## Q18: third-party lockout through the mint budget

**Decision:** reorder the gates in 1a (required). The remaining risk is a **Major** that no gate order closes, and it goes to Klas.

1. **Reorder, in 1a.** The order becomes cooldown → 10-min window → 24-h window, and each gate only runs if the one before it let the request through. The 24-h counter then counts exactly the admitted mints. "Check-all-then-increment-all" means the same thing. No Lua script is needed to hold the limit: each `INCR` is atomic. The only over-count is one 10-min slot on a request the 24-h window refuses, when the day is already spent.
   - **Why:** as written, D2:134-137 puts the budget before the cooldown and does not say how the two windows are consumed. So the 24-h counter also counts requests that mint nothing: cooled requests (D2:137) and requests the 10-min window refused.
     - In the worst reading, one burst of 10 requests delivers one mail and uses up the day. `AuthWrite` allows 20 requests/min per IP (`RateLimitingOptions.cs:31-35`).
     - After the reorder, using up the day takes 10 admitted mints. That means 10 mails to the victim over at least 30 minutes. For an existing account, each mail carries a link that stays live for 15 minutes.
   - **Lapse triggers 5 and 7:** the guess limit does not change. It is still at most 10 admitted mints per 24 h, so at most 30 guesses, which gives the same 0.003 %/day and 1.089 %/year (`:561-563`). The three quantities trigger 5 names do not change either. What changes is *what the counter counts*, and that is trigger 7 ("the budget branch changes behaviour"). Nothing has run yet, so this is an amendment before first use. It carries the re-run arithmetic, which comes out identical, and that recorded measurement is what both triggers require.
   - **Binding:** no budget key may ever exist without a TTL. Two acceptable forms:
     - `INCR` and `EXPIRE` run as one atomic unit (MULTI/EXEC or a script), or
     - `EXPIRE … NX` runs on every call, so the next call restores a missing TTL.

     A key without a TTL means that address can never log in again, and with Klas's "ingen" (D10 answer 2, `:480`) nothing would ever lift it. Built any other way, this is a Major.

2. **The residual after the reorder is a Major**, and it has a GDPR implication (Art. 32(1)(b), availability).
   - Anyone who knows an address can still keep its owner out for about 23 h a day, and repeat it every day. That includes Klas's own address. After 5a there is no password to fall back on.
   - The reorder does not lower the attack rate. With one IP at 20 requests/min, interleaving across addresses still gives about 2 locked addresses per minute per IP.
   - No gate order closes this. Any per-address limit that anyone can spend can be drained.
   - **My own error:** my 2026-09-17 Major 1 named this drain in its reasoning, as point (b). But its requirement (the ordering) only protects the live challenge against an over-budget mint. My re-check then closed Major 1 against that narrower requirement. The drain was never closed, and this residual is it.
   - Klas's D10 answer 1 accepted a mail **outage**. This is an attacker choosing whose login stops, again and again. I do not read "ingen" or "Ja det ärokej" as an answer to it.
   - **Reachability, measured:** no one outside can reach the path today.
     - The API is not exposed at the edge: `Caddyfile:2` has no `/api` matcher, and `git ls-files web/jobbliggaren-web/src/app/api` shows no catch-all route handler.
     - Every path sits behind basic_auth (`Caddyfile:176-214`).
     - It becomes reachable when part 2 connects a Server Action to `POST /auth/challenge` and basic_auth comes off.

3. **Disposition: Klas's (A) or (B).** A third option (C), keeping the lockout once real users exist, is not available under any §9.6 route. The (3) condition fails as soon as a non-controller account exists, and route (2) requires "without GDPR implication".
   - **(A) Fix it in 1a (my preference on security grounds).** Once an existing account's code budget is spent, it gets a mail with only the login link and no code. Those mails are limited only by the 10-min window and the cooldown, and a link-only record never burns the live code challenge.
     - The guess limit does not change: those mails carry no code, and the link is 128 bits (D1:106-107).
     - The victim always has a link less than 10 minutes old, or can request one.
     - The cost: the per-address mail ceiling rises from 10 per day to at most 18 per hour. That is still below the shipped password-reset path's 60 per hour (`AuthEmailCooldownOptions.cs:46-47`). Total volume stays bounded per IP.
     - **Condition (i):** the consumer writes a record for **every** request, including those over the code budget, for known and unknown addresses alike. Verify must behave the same regardless of existence: a record with no code answers Wrong, then Burned. Otherwise "link-only record for a known address, no record for an unknown one" is an existence oracle.
     - **Condition (ii):** D2's "over-budget = no-op" and my Major 1 wording are amended openly (trigger 7).
     - **Condition (iii):** a new address can still have its registration drained. That is a Minor: no account or data is at stake, and it only applies once `RegistrationsOpen=true`.
     - Design B2's resting copy already covers mails without a code (Page form `:605-608`).
     - A device-bound budget bucket (the OWASP device-cookie pattern) is the other known remedy. I do not propose it for 1a: it adds a new long-lived cookie, a register row and a WP194 §3.3 analysis, and it still leaves new devices exposed.
   - **(B) Accept the risk under §9.6 (3).** This withdraws the remedy only; the finding and its grade stay.
     - The (3) condition holds today **only if it is re-measured on the day of the decision**:
       - (i) the account table on the box;
       - (ii) the Scaleway send log, read by Klas (the 2026-08-28 precedent says a session cannot take that reading);
       - (iii) that the challenge route cannot be reached without the basic_auth credential.
     - It lapses on **any** of:
       - an account that is not Klas's exists;
       - `RegistrationsOpen=true` outside Development;
       - `POST /auth/challenge` can be reached without the basic_auth credential;
       - the (3) condition fails for any person this finding reaches.
     - Its single home is the 1a amendment of ADR 0142, and its reader is Klas.
     - I sign only once all three measurements are recorded there, with artefact and date. The remedy from (A) is still owed before go-live.
   - **Part-2 binding (Minor now, since 1a has no caller):** the Server Action must pass `forwardedHeaders()`, the way `web/jobbliggaren-web/src/lib/actions/forgot-password.ts:71` does. Otherwise the per-IP partition (`RateLimitingExtensions.cs:108-117`) collapses into one bucket behind Next (`Program.cs:331-338`, #1202), and a single client can use up every user's 20/min.

## Q2 premise: "expired instead of wrong must not change silently"

I re-measured my 2026-09-17 note (`docs/reviews/2026-09-17-auth-epic-security.md:229`).

**What holds:**
- An over-budget request writes no record (D2:134-139).
- A missing record is shown as "expired" (D3:172-173).
- A "no record → expired" answer is not an account-existence oracle, because both gates run before any account lookup and do not depend on existence (D2:135-137; `ICooldownGate.cs:11-13`).

**What I wrote that is false:** "reveals the holder's own budget status, not anyone else's."
- Budget and cooldown are keyed per **address** (`RedisCooldownGate.cs:65-69`; D1:123-125) and shared by everyone who submits that address.
- So a "no record → expired" answer tells anyone probing address X that X was submitted within the cooldown window, or is over budget. It is silent exactly when it is informative: a cooled probe sends no mail.
- This is an **address-activity signal (Minor)**. My conclusion (no existence oracle) stands; the reason I gave does not.

**What I missed:** D3's parenthesis "only expiry removes it" is false, and one of the actual causes of removal depends on whether the account exists.
- The causes of a missing record are:
  - the TTL runs out;
  - the holder's own successful verify (GETDEL);
  - a newer mint for the same address (D1:77);
  - a request that was cooled or over budget, so no record was written;
  - an enqueue dropped under load;
  - a verify that arrives before the consumer has written the record;
  - a lost keyring;
  - **the link is used.**
- Links exist only for existing accounts (D1:107-110), and using the link burns the code too (D1:105-106). So if the holder is not the mailbox owner, they see "expired" before the TTL whenever the owner clicks the link in the unsolicited mail. That is a **conditional existence signal (Minor under option (a))**: it needs the victim to click, and the mail alerts the victim.

**Binding:** the consumer writes the record before it sends the mail. Otherwise a correct code can arrive at a record that does not exist yet and get "expired".

**Q2's options, from a security view:**
- **(a) Acceptable.** The parenthesis at D3:172-173 is a false statement: delete or correct it, and name both Minors.
- **(b) Acceptable only if** "Used"/"Superseded" is told **only to someone who presents the correct code**, i.e. has proved the inbox. A tombstoned record must answer wrong codes exactly as a live one does (Wrong, then Burned on the third). An ungated "Used" gives any holder a direct existence-and-activity answer, because only a link can use someone else's challenge. **Built that way it is a Major.** State iv (`:621`) is allowed only in the gated form.
- **(c) Not preferred.** It removes only one of the two no-record causes; over-budget requests remain. It removes the window that protects a fresh challenge from being replaced by a third party right away. And it takes away the cooldown that the Q18 reorder needs.

**"Not silently" still holds:** every option above is an amendment. Remedy (A) changes the premise openly, and its condition (i) covers that.

## Q3: the budget as constants

**Decision: accepted.** Code length 6, attempts 3, TTL 15 min, 3 per 10 min and 10 per 24 h become constants in one Application home, used by both the adapter and the handlers and never typed a second time.
- These are the parameters of a measured acceptance that has lapse triggers (`:561-574`). As config keys they could be changed on the box through an env var, with no PR, no panel and no amendment, and the acceptance would lapse without a new measurement.
- The pin test asserts the ADR's literal values, and its failure message names trigger 5.

**Capacity and the cooldown window as `[Range]` options with code defaults: acceptable.** Neither enters the guess limit, because the budget caps mints whatever the cooldown is. The precedents are `AuthEmailCooldownOptions.cs:24-47` (`Range(1,3600)`) and `PasswordResetDispatchOptions.cs:33-34` (`Range(1,100_000)`). A value of 0 must stay invalid.

**§11:** a `[Range]` option with a valid default is not "a required key … a fresh dev-stack boot needs".
- Measured: neither shipped option appears in `appsettings.Local.json.example` or `local-dev-setup.md` (grep exit code 1).
- My Major 12 clause that §11 "applies to Capacity and the budget windows" assumed they would be required keys. With this form that premise is false, so the clause does not apply. The sentence at D10:469-470 should be corrected, not followed.
- If the implementation ever ends up with a required key, §11 applies to that key.

## Q20: the processing register, DoD 8, and the closed-registration copy

1. **The ADR's register bullet (`:655-658`)** names `auth/grant/v1/*` and `auth/oauth-state/v1/*`, which 1a does not create. That contradicts the ADR's own "copy follows data" at `:661-662`. It came from my own Major 9(a), which overreached against my Major 9(d).

     - `auth/challenge/v1/{id}`, TTL 15 min;

   - The in-memory dispatch queue needs no row. It lives only in memory, is bounded and is never persisted, and the reset path has no row either. It carries only the anonymised IP (D2:138).

2. **The "Utgående transaktionell e-post" entry** needs three updates.

   - The recipient classes are split: existing accounts and pending deletion are class (1); new addresses and closed registration are class (3). The register must say which variants are class (3), whatever shape Q16 picks in code.

   **The retention sentence is Major if copied.** `EmailTemplates.cs:369-370`/`:409-410` says "vi sparar den inte hos oss", and that is **false** for the login mails: the address is kept protected for up to 15 min and fingerprinted for up to 24 h. ## Q21: an account with `EmailConfirmed=false` proves its inbox by code

**Session:** yes, once the #1349 guard passes (`LoginCommandHandler.cs:81`). A correct code proves control of the inbox, which is exactly what `EmailConfirmed` certifies.

**Write `EmailConfirmed=true`:** yes, but **never on its own**.
- ADR 0127's precedent writes the flag in the same operation that replaces the password with one the inbox holder chose (`UserAccountService.cs:431-443`). It then calls `InvalidateAllForUserAsync` (`AuthEndpoints.cs:309-318`).
- A code login replaces nothing. Citing ADR 0127 while copying only the flag write copies its form without its reason.

**The attack path, in production configuration:**
1. Opening registration forces `RequireEmailConfirmation=true` (`AuthOptionsValidator.cs:74`).
2. A squatter registers the victim's address with a password. The account is unconfirmed and has no session.
3. The #714 check at `UserAccountService.cs:149` is the only thing keeping the squatter's password out.
4. If the code login writes the flag and leaves the password hash in place, the squatter's password starts passing that check, and the squatter can read the victim's CV data.

**Grades if built otherwise:**
- Flag written, password left in place: **Blocker** (auth bypass plus PII exposure).
- No flag written and nothing removed: **Major**. The squatter can trigger the unauthenticated resend, the victim's click sets the flag, and the flag-OFF dev branch leaves the squatter a live session.

**Required form:** on the first passwordless proof (code or link) for an account with `EmailConfirmed=false`:
- in one Identity write: set `EmailConfirmed=true`, set `PasswordHash` to null, and rotate `SecurityStamp`;
- then call `InvalidateAllForUserAsync` **before** `CreateAsync` (the ordering rule at `AuthEndpoints.cs:129-133`);
- write an audit row.

This must be reachable only after a Verified or consumed challenge. It must never become a bare force-confirm on `IUserAccountService`; ADR 0127 `:283-290` refused exactly that. Nulling the hash in 1a, before 1c's null-hash gate lands, is safe: the challenge path never reads lockout, and re-arming through a password reset needs the inbox.

**Accounts already confirmed:** "no data change" (D10:471 and #1735's acceptance criteria) still holds. The residual is a **Minor**:
- A squatter's password on an account the victim confirmed, by clicking an unsolicited confirmation mail, survives a code login until 5b.
- Nobody can be in that position while registration stays closed until 5b lands, which is what Klas's "Ja, radera" (before launch) implies.
- **If the #734 flip happens before 5b, this becomes a Major at the flip.** Record that condition in 1a's amendment.

No account is in that position today: `RegistrationsOpen=false` (measured 2026-09-18, #1735 comment), and both accounts have `EmailConfirmed=true` (D10:471).

## Escalation to Klas

Text for the driving session to give Klas in plain text, unabridged:

> **Fråga till Klas: en utomstående kan stänga ute en adress från inloggning (del 1a, #1735)**
>
> Koden är säker för att varje adress bara får ett begränsat antal koder: högst 3 per 10 minuter och 10 per dygn. Men budgeten räknas per e-postadress, och vem som helst som känner till en adress kan förbruka den. När den är slut kan ingen logga in på den adressen förrän dygnet har gått, och angriparen kan göra om det varje dag. Det gäller din egen adress också, och efter del 5a finns inget lösenord att falla tillbaka på.
>
> Jag kräver att ordningen i ADR 0142 rättas så att bara faktiskt skickade koder räknas. Det gör attacken långsammare och synlig (offret får tio oväntade mejl), men det stänger inte spärren.
>
> Ditt svar 2026-09-18 gällde ett mejlavbrott. Det här är något annat: en utomstående väljer vem som stängs ute och kan upprepa det. Jag läser inte ditt svar som ett svar på den här frågan.
>
> I dag kan ingen utomstående nå vägen: API:t ligger inte ut mot internet och hela sajten ligger bakom basic_auth. Det blir skarpt först när del 2 kopplar in sidan och basic_auth tas bort.
>
> Du väljer ett av två:
>
> **(A) Bygg skyddet nu, i 1a.** När kodbudgeten är slut får ett befintligt konto i stället ett mejl med bara inloggningslänken, utan kod. Länken går inte att gissa, så säkerheten för koden ändras inte, och offret har alltid en giltig länk eller kan be om en. Priset: en angripare kan skicka upp till 18 mejl i timmen till en adress i stället för 10 per dygn (dagens återställningsflöde tillåter 60 i timmen), och 1a blir något större. Nya adresser som ska registreras har ingen länk och kan fortfarande spärras ett dygn, men där finns inget konto att bli utestängd från. Det här är det jag föredrar.
>
> **(B) Godkänn risken tillfälligt, enligt CLAUDE.md §9.6 (3).** Det går bara så länge du är den enda som har konto, och godkännandet upphör så fort något av detta händer: ett konto som inte är ditt finns, registreringen öppnas, eller inloggningsvägen kan nås utan basic_auth. Skyddet måste alltså byggas före lansering ändå, bara senare. Jag signerar först när tre saker är mätta samma dag som du beslutar och skrivna i ADR 0142: kontotabellen på lådan (bara dina konton), Scaleways utskickslogg (bara till dig; den avläsningen kan bara du göra), och att inloggningsvägen inte nås utan basic_auth.
>
> Att låta spärren stå kvar när riktiga testanvändare finns är inget alternativ: då bärs risken av någon annan än du, och då kan den inte godkännas.
>
> Svara med A eller B.
